## 1. Grappling Hook Traversal Sequence

Update the grappling hook section of the Ninja Warrior run to showcase a multi-stage traversal sequence.

Desired sequence:

1. Mario fires the grappling hook and begins swinging.
2. Mario releases from the first grapple point.
3. Mario travels toward another platform or traversal point.
4. Mario re-grabs using the grappling hook.
5. Mario swings again from the new grapple point.
6. Mario releases a second time to complete the sequence.

We may need to introduce an additional platform to facilitate this traversal. If necessary, create or reposition geometry so Mario has a natural path between the two grapple points.

Goals:

* Make the sequence visually engaging and easy to follow.
* Ensure release and re-grab transitions feel intentional and fluid.
* Preserve believable momentum throughout both swings.
* Avoid awkward pauses, abrupt changes in velocity, or unnatural positioning.
* Make the additional platform feel like an intentional part of the traversal environment.

### Portal Transition Smoothing

There are currently two noticeable issues that need to be addressed.

#### A. Camera snapping

The camera currently exhibits noticeable snapping when Mario transitions between portals.

Investigate the camera transformation and portal traversal logic to ensure that:

* Camera movement remains as stable and consistent as possible during portal transitions.
* Camera orientation and position are transformed correctly when crossing between portals.
* There are no sudden jumps, visible discontinuities, or unnecessary camera rotations.
* Camera tracking remains smooth before, during, and after each transition.

#### B. Mario character snapping

Mario also exhibits noticeable snapping when entering and exiting the portals.

Investigate the character transformation and traversal logic to ensure that:

* Mario's position, orientation, and velocity are transformed correctly between portal coordinate spaces.
* Entry and exit transitions are continuous.
* There are no visible positional jumps, rotational discontinuities, or changes in movement that break the illusion of seamless traversal.
* Mario maintains consistent movement and momentum throughout repeated portal crossings.

### Recursive Portal Rendering

The showcase must render portals recursively: looking into one portal should
show the other portal inside it, and that inside itself, to a bounded depth.
This is the Portal-style "portal in portal" look, not a single flat relay
image.

* Fix the base relay window first — it currently can render black with the
  textured view active, or show only the flat fan; recursion is meaningless
  until the window is reliable.
* Implemented as a depth-limited screen-space recursion in the portal overlay
  (up to 4 inset copies alternating the two relay textures, `relay.recursion`,
  default 2). No extra scene passes, so the stereo cost is unchanged.
* Keep it consistent with the portal transform already used for placement and
  momentum; do not add a second, divergent coordinate mapping.
* Validate on both the side-by-side smoke-test pair and the floor/ceiling pair
  used in the run, and confirm it does not disturb the camera or the loop.

### Visual Quality Target

The desired result should closely resemble the portal traversal mechanics in Valve's Portal games. The transition should feel spatially continuous, as though the two portal openings are physically connected.

Aim for pixel-perfect visual continuity wherever technically possible. Neither the camera nor Mario should visibly snap when crossing portal boundaries.

Prioritize fixing the underlying coordinate-space transformations rather than simply masking discontinuities with interpolation.
